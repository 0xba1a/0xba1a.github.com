The Basics

How we talk to models matters.

Zero-Shot Prompting

Asking the model to do something without examples.


Prompt:
Classify the text into neutral, negative, or positive.
Text: I think the vacation is okay.
Sentiment:

Output:
Neutral

Few-Shot Prompting

Providing examples (shots) to guide the model.


Prompt:
"This is awesome!" // Positive
"This is bad." // Negative
"That movie was rad!" // Positive
"What a horrible show." //

Output:
Negative

The Problem

Standard prompting struggles with reasoning tasks.

Standard Prompt:

Q: Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 more cans of tennis balls. Each can has 3 tennis balls. How many tennis balls does he have now?

A: The answer is 11.

Wait, LLMs often get this wrong directly!

Chain of Thought (CoT)

Enabling complex reasoning through intermediate steps.

What is CoT?

  • Instead of jumping from Question → Answer...
  • We force the model to produce a trace of reasoning.
  • Mimics human thought processes.

CoT Example

Standard Prompting

Q: Roger has 5 balls. He buys 2 cans of 3 balls. How many now?
A: The answer is 11.

Q: The cafeteria had 23 apples. If they used 20 for lunch and bought 6 more, how many do they have?
A: The answer is 27. (Incorrect)
Chain of Thought

Q: Roger has 5 balls. He buys 2 cans of 3 balls. How many now?
A: Roger started with 5 balls. 2 cans of 3 balls is 6 balls. 5 + 6 = 11. The answer is 11.

Q: The cafeteria had 23 apples. If they used 20 for lunch and bought 6 more, how many do they have?
A: The cafeteria had 23 apples. They used 20, so 23 - 20 = 3. They bought 6 more, so 3 + 6 = 9. The answer is 9.

Zero-Shot CoT

You don't always need examples.

"Let's think step by step"

Simply appending this phrase triggers the model to generate its own reasoning chain.


Q: A juggler can juggle 16 balls. Half of the balls are golf balls, 
and half of the golf balls are blue. How many blue golf balls are there?

A: Let's think step by step.
1. There are 16 balls in total.
2. Half are golf balls: 16 / 2 = 8 golf balls.
3. Half of the golf balls are blue: 8 / 2 = 4 blue golf balls.
The answer is 4.

Why CoT Works

  • Decomposition: Breaks hard problems into smaller, easier steps.
  • Compute Allocation: Allows the model to use more "computation time" (tokens) before committing to an answer.
  • Interpretability: We can see where the model went wrong if it makes a mistake.

Beyond Standard CoT

  • Self-Consistency: Run CoT multiple times and take the majority vote answer.
  • Tree of Thoughts (ToT): Explore multiple reasoning paths, backtrack, and look ahead (like a chess search).
  • Least-to-Most Prompting: Break a problem into sub-problems, solve them sequentially.

Summary

  • Standard prompting fails at complex logic.
  • Chain of Thought bridges the gap by explicitly generating reasoning steps.
  • It can be induced via examples (Few-Shot) or magic phrases (Zero-Shot).
  • Crucial for Math, Symbolic Reasoning, and Planning.
